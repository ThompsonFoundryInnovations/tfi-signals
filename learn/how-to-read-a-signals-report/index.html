How to read a Signals report

A report is written in plain sentences. Here is what each part shows, using one of our own.

Last updated: October 10, 2026 Source: how TFI Signals works today About a 4 minute read

Signals is still being designed and built. This guide describes the report as it works today. Some things will change and new features are being added soon, and this guide will be updated to match.

A report as it looks today, simplified for display. The business is our own.

The report, from the top

  1. The heading. The business name, its city, and why we matched the report to it. It also says this is an early version, when the check ran, and has a Check again button.
  2. How to read this page. Every finding is written as a plain sentence. The label beside it says how much it matters. Small numbers match the numbered sources, and "How we know this" shows which sources said it and the date they were read.
  3. The summary. What we found overall, what needs attention, and what to do about it.
  4. Business profile. The category, address, phone numbers and website we found. If we found only part of something, such as just the state, it says so.
  5. Possible matches. Results with a similar name that might or might not be this business. None is confirmed, and nothing else on the page was read from them. Results we judged to be a different business or a person's profile are counted but not shown.
  6. Website checks. A mobile speed score out of 100 for the website most sources point to. Google scores a page out of 100: 70 or more is good, 50 to 69 could be faster, and under 50 is slow. Scores vary a little from run to run.
  7. Checks, findings and sources. A count of the public checks that ran, then the findings they were grouped into, an explanation of the numbers, and the list of sources behind the findings.
  8. Add a link of your own. You can add a page you know belongs to the business, such as its Facebook page. The link stays in your browser only. It is not saved, no check reads it, and only you can see it.

The count at the bottom

Each report says how many public checks ran and what happened with each one.

  • Found somethingThe check turned up information about the business.
  • Found nothingThe check ran and did not find it. See "What 'we did not find it' means."
  • Did not applyThe check was not relevant to this business.
  • Could not be checkedWe could not get an answer at all, so we say nothing about whether the information exists.

What a report cannot tell you

  • It reads public information only. It never signs in to anything and does not change your business or any listing.
  • Results can be incomplete or mistaken. Some pages, such as Facebook and LinkedIn, cannot be read directly, so a profile that is not shown may simply be one we could not see.
  • It does not judge your business. It shows what public sources say at the time of the check.
  • The list of sources does not yet include the pages behind possible matches or checks that found nothing.

Want to see one for your business? Type in a business name and city. A check usually takes one to three minutes.
